# Commercial Property Lending — Credit Policy

> **Fictional document.** Written for the LoanLens demo. It does not describe any real lender's policy, and none of the figures are a recommendation.

| | |
|---|---|
| Version | 1.0 |
| Applies to | First-mortgage commercial property loans arranged through brokers |
| Owner | Head of Credit |

Sections marked **[System check]** are tested automatically by LoanLens with deterministic rules. All other sections are matters of credit judgement: LoanLens may point to them as possible risks, but only a credit officer decides whether they are met.

---

## §1 Purpose and principles

§1.1 This policy sets the minimum standards for commercial property loans. Meeting it does not oblige the Lender to make a loan.

§1.2 Every loan is approved by a credit manager. Automated tools may extract information and point out risks; they never approve or decline an application.

§1.3 Any departure from this policy must be named in the credit submission, with reasons, and approved by a credit manager.

---

## §2 Eligible borrowers

§2.1 The Lender lends to companies, trusts, individuals and self-managed superannuation funds (SMSFs).

§2.2 **[System check]** A borrower that supplies an Australian Business Number (ABN) must supply a valid one. An ABN that fails its check-digit test must be resolved before approval.

§2.3 A borrower entity formed less than 12 months before the application needs additional review: the credit submission should identify the people behind the entity, their experience, and any guarantees offered.

§2.4 For an SMSF borrower, the loan must be made under a limited recourse borrowing arrangement, the property must be held in a separate holding trust, and the fund must have taken independent legal and financial advice. Personal guarantees from the fund members are expected.

---

## §3 Loan size and term

§3.1 **[System check]** The loan amount must be at least $500,000 and no more than $50,000,000. Applications outside this range need a credit manager's approval as a policy exception.

§3.2 **[System check]** The loan term must be no longer than 36 months.

§3.3 Bridging loans should be short. The credit submission must show how the bridging loan will be repaid within its term.

---

## §4 Loan-to-value ratio (LVR)

§4.1 LVR is the loan amount divided by the property value in the current valuation, expressed as a percentage. Where a document states its own LVR, the Lender's calculation from the loan amount and the valuation is the one that counts.

§4.2 **[System check]** The maximum LVR depends on the security:

| Security | Maximum LVR |
|---|---|
| Completed income-producing property: office, retail, industrial or mixed use | 65% |
| Construction or residential development (any loan whose purpose is construction, or any residential development site) | 60% |
| Vacant land | 50% |

§4.3 **[System check]** Where more than one limit in §4.2 could apply, the lowest one applies.

§4.4 **[System check]** If the LVR stated in the application differs from the Lender's calculation by more than 2 percentage points, the difference must be explained before approval. A mismatch usually means the application uses a different valuation or loan amount from the one supplied.

---

## §5 Valuation

§5.1 **[System check]** Every application must be supported by a valuation of the security property. An application without one cannot proceed.

§5.2 **[System check]** The valuation must be dated no more than 6 months before the application is assessed. An older valuation must be updated or re-addressed to the Lender.

§5.3 Valuations should be prepared by an independent valuer on the Lender's panel. A valuation commissioned by the borrower may be accepted for initial assessment only.

§5.4 Where the valuation relies on a single tenant, the credit submission should comment on the tenant's strength and the remaining lease term.

---

## §6 Construction and development loans

§6.1 **[System check]** A residential development loan must have qualifying presales covering at least 50% of the development before the first drawdown.

§6.2 The builder must hold a fixed-price building contract and appropriate licences and insurance. The credit submission should comment on the builder's track record.

§6.3 The development budget should include a contingency of at least 5% of construction costs. A smaller contingency is a matter for credit judgement.

§6.4 Funds are released in progress payments certified by an independent quantity surveyor.

---

## §7 Exit strategy

§7.1 Every loan must have a clear, credible way to be repaid by the end of its term: sale of the property, refinance with another lender, or repayment from other identified funds.

§7.2 An exit that depends on a single buyer, a single tenant, or an event that has not yet happened (such as a rezoning or a development approval) carries higher risk and must be explained in the credit submission.

§7.3 Where the exit is refinance, the credit submission should show that the loan would meet a mainstream lender's criteria at the end of the term.

---

## §8 Required information

§8.1 **[System check]** An application must state at least: the borrower's name and type, the loan amount, the loan purpose, the loan term, the security property's address and type, and the property value. Missing items must be obtained from the broker before assessment continues.

§8.2 Applications should also state the broker, any existing debt secured on the property, the valuer and valuation date, the exit strategy, and, for development loans, the level of presales.

---

## §9 Approval and records

§9.1 A credit manager approves or declines every application. Approval must be recorded with the approver's name, the date and any conditions.

§9.2 Every critical risk must be acknowledged by the credit officer before an application is sent for approval.

§9.3 The Lender keeps a record of the original application documents, the information extracted from them, any corrections made by staff, and the approval decision.
